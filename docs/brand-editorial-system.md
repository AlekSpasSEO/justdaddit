# JustDaddit brand and editorial system

## The short version

JustDaddit helps US families choose expensive gear for adventures, projects, travel, and outdoor play. It is written by Aleksandar Spasevski, a father and researcher who values useful weekends over impressive specifications.

The promise is simple: do the homework, show the trade-offs, calculate the real starting cost, and never pretend a product was tested when it was not.

## Brand soul

JustDaddit is the friend who reads the manual before everyone meets in the driveway. He is enthusiastic, but not excitable. He understands why a child wants the fastest version and why a parent cares about replacement parts, storage, noise, supervision, and whether the thing will still work next month.

The brand believes:

1. Family gear is bought for a memory, not for a specification sheet.
2. The hidden cost is often more important than the advertised price.
3. Repairable beats disposable.
4. A clear "skip it" is as valuable as a recommendation.
5. Safety belongs in the decision, without turning every article into anxious parenting content.
6. Research has to be transparent. A desk review is useful when it is labeled honestly and built from strong evidence.

## Reader

The primary reader is a US parent, usually shopping for children aged 4 to 12, who wants to buy once and buy intelligently. They are short on time, suspicious of generic affiliate roundups, and willing to spend more when the reason is concrete.

They need answers to questions such as:

- Will my child actually be able to use this?
- What else must I buy before the first weekend?
- How much space does it need at home and in the car?
- What breaks, and can I get the part?
- Is the premium version meaningfully better?
- Will this become another large object nobody uses?

## Voice

The voice is candid, practical, curious, warm, and occasionally dry. It sounds like a capable father explaining a purchase to another parent after doing more research than either of them expected.

Use:

- Short verdicts before long explanations
- Concrete family situations
- First person for judgment, never for invented use
- Plain descriptions of technical systems
- Specific trade-offs and clear buyer groups
- Calm humor that comes from recognition
- Phrases such as "the dad math," "the first-weekend cost," "the annoying bit," and "who should skip it" when they fit naturally

Avoid:

- Baby-talk, macho posturing, and "dad bod" clichés
- Corporate optimism and breathless product hype
- Empty words such as revolutionary, game-changing, ultimate, must-have, and perfect
- Calling a product tested, proven, or family-approved without a documented protocol
- Treating owner anecdotes as failure-rate data
- Rewriting retailer descriptions
- Fake urgency and evergreen prices presented as current facts
- The words "surfaces" and "moat"
- Em dashes

## Vocabulary

Preferred nouns: family, parent, child, weekend, driveway, yard, trip, project, setup, repair, parts, battery, storage, supervision, trade-off, evidence, owner report, manual, starting cost.

Preferred verbs: choose, compare, repair, carry, charge, store, supervise, verify, skip, outgrow, maintain.

Use "children" in formal safety or age guidance. Use "kids" in conversational copy. Never use a child as a prop in a product claim.

## Evidence labels

Every commercial page carries one label:

- **Research Review:** no JustDaddit hands-on use. Built from manufacturer documents, independent evaluation, owner evidence, parts availability, retailer data, and recall checks.
- **Owner-Verified:** a product owner has supplied structured observations, photos, and ownership details, but JustDaddit did not run a full test protocol.
- **Hands-On:** JustDaddit used the exact product under a documented protocol.
- **Long-Term:** the exact product has been used for at least six months with maintenance and failure notes.

The prototype uses Research Review only.

## Review scoring

The overall score is an editorial decision, not an average disguised as science. Six category scores support it:

1. Family fit
2. Control and learning curve
3. Durability and repairability
4. Setup and ownership
5. Performance for the intended use
6. Value at the dated reference price

Every score needs a linked explanation. Scores can change when price, generation, parts support, or evidence changes.

## Required review structure

1. Evidence label, update date, and exact model identity
2. Compact verdict with price context, best for, skip if, and overall score
3. Author card and research disclosure
4. Six quick answers
5. Category scorecard
6. The family decision and realistic use case
7. First-weekend setup and full starting cost
8. Performance translated into usable space and supervision
9. Durability, likely wear items, and parts support
10. Storage, transport, charging, cleanup, and maintenance
11. Owner-feedback patterns with limitations
12. Simple comparison and expanded specifications
13. Alternatives with linked internal pages
14. Visible FAQs
15. Methodology, source ledger, recall check, and update history
16. Final buy/skip verdict

Target depth: roughly 4,000 to 5,500 useful words when the product and intent justify it. No padding.

## Hub structure

Each hub begins with a selector or decision framework. It explains category vocabulary, total ownership cost, safety and supervision, repairability, where products can be used, and how the recommendations were selected. Product cards must state both "best for" and "skip if."

## Comparison structure

Every comparison names the decision in the first screen. It then compares total starting cost, intended user, controllability, performance, durability, maintenance, ecosystem, and the situations where each product wins. A comparison cannot be a duplicated pair of mini reviews.

## Images

Use up to three editorial images per article. Prefer documented owner photos, community images with permission, original photography, or manufacturer/retailer assets with a clear usage record. Every image needs useful alternative text, dimensions, credit, source URL, and source type. Do not generate a fake image of a real product.

## Affiliate and safety rules

- Put an affiliate disclosure before the first commercial link.
- Mark affiliate links `nofollow sponsored noopener`.
- Date prices and availability.
- Check the CPSC recall database before publication and during scheduled updates.
- Link manuals and warranties where possible.
- Do not recommend safety-critical substitutions or unauthorized modifications.
- State battery and charging risks in practical language.

## Author identity

Public author: Aleksandar Spasevski, founder of JustDaddit, father, marketer, and obsessive pre-purchase researcher. His children remain private. The founder biography should explain the reason for the project without borrowing credibility from work he has not done.

## Design character

The visual system should feel like a well-used field notebook crossed with a modern product lab. Warm paper, deep ink, safety orange, electric lime, large editorial typography, compact data blocks, visible ruled lines, and practical labels. It should never resemble a pastel parenting blog or a generic Amazon storefront.
